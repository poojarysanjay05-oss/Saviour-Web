How the 50/30/20 rule works

The 50/30/20 rule is a simple way to allocate your take-home pay without tracking every category in detail. Half goes to essentials, less than a third to lifestyle spending, and a fifth to your future self.

Needs = Income × 0.5, Wants = Income × 0.3, Savings = Income × 0.2

Example: on a ₹50,000 take-home income, that's ₹25,000 for needs, ₹15,000 for wants, and ₹10,000 for savings.

Tips to make the split work

  • Pay yourself first — move the 20% to savings before spending on anything else.
  • If needs run over 50%, target your biggest fixed cost (usually rent) first.
  • Track wants loosely with a weekly cap rather than itemising every purchase.
  • Revisit the split whenever your income or rent changes.

Frequently asked questions

What is the 50/30/20 rule?

The 50/30/20 rule splits your take-home income into 50% for needs, 30% for wants, and 20% for savings and debt repayment.

What counts as a need vs a want?

Needs are essentials like rent, groceries, utilities and minimum debt payments. Wants are discretionary spending like dining out, entertainment and shopping.

What if my needs are more than 50% of income?

If needs exceed 50%, look at reducing fixed costs like rent or loan payments, or temporarily lower the wants and savings percentages until income grows.

Is 20% savings always enough?

20% is a solid baseline, but if you have specific goals like retirement or a home down payment, saving more than 20% will get you there faster.
